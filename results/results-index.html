How to read these numbers

  • Leading is not winning. While counting is live, cards say "leading." A measure or race is only marked decided when the AP calls it, and the card will say so. Final official results come with certification, by December 11 for the state and November 27 for the county.
  • Precincts reporting is not ballots counted. Each card shows the share of precincts that have reported. California does not publish a running total of ballots left to count on election night, and mail ballots postmarked by Election Day keep arriving until November 10. Early percentages lean heavily on mail and early voters, and late counts can move close contests.
  • Percentages are of votes counted so far on that measure or race, not of all ballots cast. The margin shown is the raw vote gap between the two sides.
  • Two sources, two clocks. The state feed updates continuously through the night. The county releases results in batches, so local numbers move in steps and may lag the state numbers.
